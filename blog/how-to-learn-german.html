How Do You Learn German Best?

Why my lessons never spend a minute on what you can do on your own – and how that helps you progress faster

Reading time: approx. 8 minutes

🇩🇪 Diesen Artikel auf Deutsch lesen

Amy Lang

German teacher with almost 20 years of teaching experience, Master’s degree (Mag.) in Communication Science and Applied Linguistics (Freie Universität Berlin)

There is no single path on which everyone learns German equally well. One person first needs a solid grammatical framework before she dares to speak. Another just starts talking and only afterwards wants to know why a sentence is built the way it is. That is why I don’t have a rigid schedule that pushes every learner through the same steps. Every time, I adapt completely to you: to your goal, your pace and the point you have reached right now.

In this article I’d like to show you how my lessons work, which two paths my students take most often, and what you can do between lessons so that your German really grows.

What you can do alone, you do at home

My lessons are very intensive and compact. In concrete terms, this means that we don’t sit together in class reading long texts or listening to podcasts. You can do that perfectly well on your own, at your own pace and as often as you like. Our time together is too precious to fill it with things you don’t need me for.

In class, we do what you cannot do on your own. I explain what needs explaining, I give you instructions and I show you the way. In every session I take you exactly as far as I need to, until I know that you won’t lose your way before the next lesson. From that point on, you can keep working independently – as much as you like.

I accompany you exactly to the point where you can safely continue on your own. From there, you decide how fast you move forward.

For the same reason, we don’t stubbornly work through one textbook chapter after another. We focus on what you need right now. If nothing is missing in chapter 3 but there is a gap in chapter 9, we jump to where real progress happens for you.

Two paths to mastering German

When you come to my lessons, you usually have one of two goals. Both are equally valuable, and over time they often merge all by themselves.

01

You want to understand the grammar

You finally want to know why it’s “dem” and not “den”, when the verb moves to the end of the sentence and how to switch confidently between tenses.

02

You want to speak with confidence

You already know a lot, but when it really counts, the words won’t come – or you’re unsure whether your sentences come across correctly at all.

Path 1: Grammar – my speciality

Grammar is what I do best. I teach it to you as fast as you can grasp it, and not a minute slower. You won’t have to struggle through tedious repetition if you understood something long ago. And if a topic takes longer, we take that time until it really sits.

Once you have understood the principle, you can practise at home as much as you like. You get the right exercises from me and know exactly what to pay attention to. In the next lesson we clear up anything that is still open and take the next step.

Path 2: Speaking – with confidence instead of a racing heart

Hardly anyone starts a discussion in public or in an important situation on a topic they feel unsure about. Imagine you are supposed to talk about geopolitics in a foreign language, but you’re not sure whether you have the vocabulary or whether you can put your sentences together so that people understand you at all. Most people would rather stay silent.

That is exactly what you can practise with me, in a safe setting and with a clear structure. Many of my students begin every lesson by speaking. This is how it works:

1

You speak

For the first 15 to 20 minutes, you talk about a topic. It can be a prepared topic or a spontaneous one – whatever you prefer.

2

I take notes

I write down everything exactly as you said it. If you wish, I give you feedback along the way; otherwise I let you speak without interruption.

3

I analyse everything

After the lesson, in addition to your booked session, you receive a PDF of several pages with a complete analysis of my notes on what you said.

What your analysis contains

  • Useful phrases for the topics you talked about, so that next time you sound more precise and more natural.
  • An analysis of your grammar, sentence by sentence, with corrections and explanations.
  • Your personal error patterns: Which mistakes keep coming back? Where are you particularly prone to errors? You see in black and white what you should work on.

Your analysis becomes your learning plan

The beauty of this method is that the analysis immediately tells us which grammar topics you need to work on. We don’t have to guess, and we don’t have to follow the order of a textbook. Your own speaking shows us the way.

That is why I work like this with some of my students: at the beginning, we get two good grammar books. After each analysis, I tell you exactly which chapter to work on at home, because that very topic is currently causing the biggest stumbling blocks in your speaking. If necessary, I briefly explain the chapter in class first so that you can start with confidence.

This combination of free speaking, precise analysis and targeted grammar work has proven to be the most effective method in my teaching so far. You learn exactly what you are missing, and from week to week you notice your speaking becoming more confident.

Between lessons: let German into your everyday life

The lessons are the engine, but the driving happens in everyday life. That’s why I recommend that you use German actively as often as possible. Here are my most important tips.

🎬 Films and series in two stages

If you speak English well, you can use it as a bridge at the beginning. Then step it up gradually:

Stage 1

German + English

German audio with English subtitles, or the other way round.

Stage 2

All in German

German audio and German subtitles: you hear and read the same words at the same time.

📖 Books you can enjoy from level B1

Many learners wait far too long before reading real novels. From level B1 you can already start – with books that are good literature and fun to read at the same time. I especially like to recommend these two German novels:

Gut gegen Nordwind

Daniel Glattauer

A novel told in e-mails: short sections, everyday language and a story that makes you desperate to know what happens next.

Tschick

Wolfgang Herrndorf

A road trip of two teenagers through eastern Germany, told in a lively and humorous way, with lots of spoken language.

🎧 Podcasts about your interests

Look for German podcasts on topics that genuinely interest you, whether that’s cooking, football, psychology, technology or history. You learn to speak fastest about the things that excite you, because you really need those words and enjoy using them.

Never underestimate your motivation. What interests you sticks, and what you love talking about is what you learn to talk about fastest.

Your path is as individual as you are

Whether you start with grammar, with speaking or with both at once: what matters is that you follow your own path, with someone at your side who helps you exactly where you can’t move on alone. Everything else you can do yourself – and you can do more than you might think.
